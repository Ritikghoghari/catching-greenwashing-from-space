Verifying Palm Oil Zero-Deforestation Commitments with Satellite Observation & NLP

Do corporate zero-deforestation commitments match physical reality on the ground? This research audits 11 global palm oil conglomerates across 290 supply chain mills, computing an empirical 0–100 Mismatch Score that cross-examines NLP-extracted sustainability claims against satellite forest-loss data under the EUDR framework.

Understanding This Research

What Is This Project Studying?

Many large palm oil companies promise "zero deforestation" in their annual sustainability reports. This research checks whether those promises match reality — by combining satellite observations of actual forest loss with AI-powered analysis of corporate language. A higher Mismatch Score means a larger gap between what a company claims and what satellites detect.

🌍

Where & What?

11 palm oil conglomerates, 290 processing mills across Indonesia, Malaysia, and West Africa. Forest loss is measured within a 10 km radius of each mill — the typical area from which mills source fruit.

📅

When?

Loss is counted from 1 January 2021 onwards — the cutoff date set by the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR). Companies were required to be deforestation-free by 31 December 2020 to sell into the EU.

🛰️

How Was It Measured?

Satellite data from the Hansen Global Forest Change dataset (Landsat 30 m, annual) detects where tree cover disappeared. Each mill's catchment area is processed through Google Earth Engine.

🤖

What Did NLP Add?

Five AI language models read 403 corporate sustainability statements and scored them on specificity (concrete vs. vague) and sentiment (promotional tone). High specificity + high sentiment + high loss = high mismatch.

📊

What Is the Mismatch Score?

A 0–100 index where higher = greater discrepancy between claims and satellite evidence. It weights forest loss (35%), claim specificity (35%), promotional sentiment (15%), and spatial distribution (15%).

⚠️

What Can't We Conclude?

Proximity to a mill does not prove the company caused the deforestation. Smallholders and third parties also operate nearby. This measures spatial correspondence, not legal responsibility.

Dual-Track Verification Stack

9 Machine Learning Models & Empirical Accuracy Benchmark

Our methodology enforces strict isolation between textual claim extraction and satellite ground truth. 5 NLP Transformer models audit corporate sustainability disclosures while 4 geospatial and vision AI systems quantify physical canopy loss across 290 mill catchments.

Track 1: Textual Claims Audit

5 NLP Transformer Models

NLP Benchmark Stack
1. climatebert/environmental-claims Acc: 97.3% · F1: 0.981

DistilRoBERTa model detecting genuine zero-deforestation commitments. Precision: 100%, Recall: 96.2% on gold palm oil benchmark (n=150).

Baseline comparison: Keyword regex achieved only 70.7% Acc and 0.828 F1.
2. climatebert/climate-specificity Acc: 53.8% · Score: 0–100

DistilRoBERTa scoring commitment concreteness. Found an even 50/50 split between concrete targets and vague promotional talk across 403 claims.

Continuous score directly calibrates the specificity dimension in the mismatch formula.
3. ProsusAI/finbert Acc: 81.1%

Domain-adapted financial BERT quantifying promotional cheerfulness in corporate disclosures. Directly feeds the mismatch score formula (weight 0.15).

Baseline comparison: Standard lexicon sentiment achieved 62.4% accuracy.
4. climatebert/climate-sentiment Acc: 50.9% · 99.8% Opportunity

SASB climate framing classifier showing that 99.8% of palm oil corporate claims frame climate change as an opportunity rather than an operational risk.

Baseline comparison: Generic sentiment lexicons scored 44.1% accuracy.
5. nbroad/ESG-BERT Acc: 80.2%

Multi-class BERT categorizing claims into official SASB ESG pillars (Land Use & Biodiversity, GHG Emissions, Labor Practices).

Baseline comparison: Keyword rule tags achieved only 18.9% accuracy.
Track 2: Physical Ground Truth

4 Satellite & Vision Systems

Remote Sensing Stack
1. Hansen Global Forest Change v1.13 99.5% Global Acc

Landsat 30m time-series random forest model. Detected 890,168 ha post-2020 forest loss across all 290 mills in 10 km radial catchments post-EUDR cutoff.

Tropical user accuracy: 88.0%. Detected loss across 100% of the 290 mills.
2. Descals Global 10m Oil Palm CNN 86.9% Overall Acc

Multi-sensor deep CNN fusing Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 optical imagery. Confirmed 56.0% of all post-2020 buffer clearing was converted directly into oil palm plantations (reaches 89.8% for IOI).

Attribution proof: Forest clearings were turned into palm estates, not subsistence crops.
3. RADD Radar Disturbance Alerts >95% User Acc

Sentinel-1 C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) alerts piercing equatorial cloud cover and monsoon haze. Confirmed active weekly clearing at 4 of the 5 highest-loss mills.

All-weather validation: Confirms that optical clearing signals are not cloud artifacts.
4. NASA/IBM Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M + Probe ρ = 0.805 · IoU: 0.765

300M Vision Transformer (ViT) spatial foundation model. While zero-shot baseline achieved ρ = 0.597 (ρ = 0.172 network mean), our Supervised Linear Probe on frozen embeddings achieved ρ = 0.805, 89.7% precision, 83.9% recall, and 76.5% IoU.

Breakthrough: +34.8% correlation gain without expensive supercomputer retraining.

Research Limitations

What This Study Cannot Conclude

These limitations are documented in the thesis (Chapter 5) and must be considered when interpreting any finding. The Mismatch Score measures a statistical signal — it does not constitute legal evidence of greenwashing.

🏭

Proximity ≠ Causation

Forest loss is measured within 10 km of each mill. Smallholder farmers, other companies, and government concessions also operate in the same area. The study measures spatial correspondence, not proven legal responsibility for clearing.

📡

30 m Resolution Cap

Hansen GFC uses Landsat at 30 m per pixel. Clearings smaller than one pixel (~0.09 ha) may be missed. High-frequency small-scale clearing, common in smallholder areas, is likely under-detected.

☁️

Cloud Cover in Tropics

Equatorial regions have persistent cloud cover. While the Hansen model uses multi-year composites to reduce cloud artifacts, some loss events may be missed or dated inaccurately in high-cloud years.

📄

NLP Claim Availability

KLK (8 claims) and Wilmar (4 claims) had very few accessible English-language sustainability disclosures for the study period. Low claim counts reduce the reliability of their NLP sub-scores and are flagged in the data.

🗺️

10 km Buffer Choice

The 10 km catchment boundary is a methodological choice, not a physical law. Buffer sensitivity analysis (5–30 km) confirms rank stability up to 20 km (Spearman ρ = 0.80–0.96), but the 30 km buffer dilutes results (ρ = 0.49) by capturing unrelated concessions.

🏷️

Mismatch Score Is Not a Legal Finding

A high score signals a discrepancy between corporate language and satellite observations — not proven fraud or a regulatory violation. Independent legal audits, supply-chain traceability data, and concession maps would be required to make regulatory claims.
