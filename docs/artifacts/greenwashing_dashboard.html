01 · Plain-language summary

What this data shows

Eleven palm oil companies publish sustainability reports that say some version of "we have zero deforestation" or "we are deforestation-free." This project checks that claim against satellite evidence, for every mill each company operates, for the years after the cutoff date set by the EU Deforestation Regulation (31 December 2020).

The result: across 290 mills belonging to these 11 companies, 890,168 hectares of forest were lost after the 2020 cutoff, inside a 10 km radius of the mills. That is roughly the land area of Cyprus. The companies making the strongest "zero deforestation" language are not automatically the ones with the least measured loss — in several cases they are the opposite. That mismatch, between the words in the report and the pixels from the satellite, is what this thesis measures and calls the mismatch score.

This dashboard is built from the actual pipeline output, not illustrative numbers. Every figure below traces back to a CSV in Results/ or data/, reproducible from the scripts in scripts/.

02 · How the numbers were produced

The pipeline: text in, evidence out

Two independent measurements are made for each company, then combined into one score. Neither measurement depends on the other, which is what makes the comparison meaningful.

STEP 1

NLP claim extraction

A ClimateBERT-based model (Webersinke et al., 2022; Stammbach et al., 2022) reads each company's sustainability report and pulls out sentences that make a deforestation-related claim. Each claim is scored 0-1 for specificity — a vague line like "we care about forests" scores low, a measurable line like "zero deforestation across all operations since 2020" scores high. Bingler et al. (2022) call vague corporate language "cheap talk"; the specificity score is built to catch exactly that.

STEP 2

Satellite verification

For every mill belonging to the company, Hansen Global Forest Change data (Hansen et al., 2013, Science 342(6160)) is queried inside a 10 km radius, the standard fresh-fruit-bunch sourcing catchment used in palm oil supply-chain mapping. Forest loss is measured pixel-by-pixel for every year since 2001, then split at the EUDR cutoff date so pre- and post-2020 loss can be compared separately.

STEP 3

Mismatch score

The claim-specificity score and the measured post-2020 loss (normalized per mill) are combined into one 0-100 mismatch score per company. A high score means: strong, specific "zero deforestation" language, paired with real measured loss near that company's own mills.

04 · Is it getting better or worse

Year-by-year trends

A single post-2020 total hides whether clearing stopped after the commitment was made, or kept going. These two case studies show it kept going.

IOI — worst-intensity company, year by year

Loss roughly doubled from 2021 (10,115 ha) to 2024 (20,359 ha), before dropping in the partial 2025 window. The worst individual mills — SYARIMO, LEEPANG, and MORISEM / LADANG SABAH — were still climbing as of 2024.

Wilmar Sabahmas — one mill, five years

A single Wilmar mill, chosen because it shows continuous clearing rather than a one-off event.

05 · Data quality, shown not hidden

A bug we found and fixed

The mill list (UML — Universal Mill List) records each mill's owning group under two possible columns: Group Name and Parent Company. The first version of the matching script only searched Group Name. For SD Guthrie, most of its mills are listed under Parent Company instead, so the first pass matched only 2 mills.

Searching both columns raised that to 42 mills, which changed SD Guthrie's total post-2020 loss from a near-zero placeholder to 142,131 ha, its correct position in the ranking above. This kind of matching error is easy to miss because the script runs without an error message — it just silently returns too little data. The fix and the before/after numbers are kept here deliberately, since a thesis that catches other companies under-reporting should be transparent about its own data corrections.

06 · What this data does not prove

Limitations, stated honestly

This is a proximity proxy, not causal attribution

Forest loss measured within a mill's 10 km catchment cannot be causally attributed to that mill's operator. Other actors — smallholders, other plantations, roads, fires, illegal clearing — can and do operate in the same area. This dashboard measures correspondence between a company's claims and forest loss near its mills, not causation. PalmWatch, whose methodology this project follows for the 10 km buffer, documents the same limitation in its own work.

The mill catchment is an approximation

10 km is a standard estimate of how far a mill sources fresh fruit bunches from, not a verified supply-chain boundary for any specific mill. Real sourcing radii vary by geography, road access, and company logistics.

Claim extraction depends on report language and translation

The NLP model reads what is written in each company's report. A company that simply avoids making any hard claim (as Wilmar and Genting do, at 0% claim strength) will show a low mismatch score even if satellite loss near its mills is high — because there is no specific claim for the loss to formally contradict. Low mismatch does not mean low impact.

Coverage varies by company

Some companies have far more mills matched in the UML than others (SD Guthrie's 42 versus SIPEF's 11), which affects how representative the per-mill averages are. This is noted per company in the underlying scoring output.

07 · Research grounding

Papers behind the method

  1. Stammbach, D. et al. (2022). NLP model for climate claim classification. arXiv:2209.00507. Used for the claim-extraction step.
  2. Webersinke, N. et al. (2022). ClimateBERT: A pretrained language model for climate-related text. arXiv:2110.12010. Base model underlying the claim classifier.
  3. Hansen, M.C. et al. (2013). High-resolution global maps of 21st-century forest cover change. Science, 342(6160). Source of every forest-loss hectare figure on this page.
  4. Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 (EUDR). Sets the 31 December 2020 cutoff date used throughout this project.
  5. PalmWatch methodology. Basis for the 10 km mill-catchment buffer and the proximity-proxy limitation stated above.
  6. Calamai, et al. (2026). arXiv:2502.07541. Motivates the research gap and shaped the two-sided methodology design.
  7. Ong, et al. (2025). arXiv:2502.15821. Identifies the missing verification step this thesis adds — extraction alone, without satellite checking, is not new; the combination is.
  8. Bingler, J. et al. (2022). On "cheap talk" in corporate climate disclosures. Motivates scoring claims by specificity rather than presence alone.
