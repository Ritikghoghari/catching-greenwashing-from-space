Step 3 — PDF Report Collection

Annual sustainability and NDPE reports downloaded directly from company websites. Verified against SPOTT for completeness and currency.

Tool: pdfplumber (Python) — extracts text page by page. Regex cleaning applied to fix broken sentences from PDF column layouts.

Challenge: PDFs are unstructured — tables, headers, and multi-column layouts break sentences. This is a known limitation of PDF-based NLP.

Step 4 — NLP Claim Extraction (5 Models)

Five ClimateBERT/BERT-family models run in sequence on every sentence from every sustainability report.

Model 1 (Stammbach 2022): climatebert/environmental-claims — flags real environmental commitments vs general language.

Model 2 (Webersinke 2022): climate-specificity — scores each claim 0–1. Near 1.0 = measurable, dated, checkable. Near 0 = vague aspiration.

Model 3 (Araci 2019): ProsusAI/finbert — financial sentiment (positive/neutral/negative). Feeds directly into the mismatch score as sentiment_score.

Model 4: climatebert/distilroberta-base-climate-sentiment — climate framing (risk/neutral/opportunity). Used as comparison signal.

Model 5: nbroad/ESG-BERT — classifies each claim into SASB ESG category.

Result: 403 enriched claims (claims_enriched.csv) across 11 companies. FinBERT and climate-sentiment agree on only 53% of claims (κ=0.078) — they measure different things; FinBERT used in the mismatch formula, climate-sentiment reported separately.

Step 5 — Mill Location Matching

Universal Mill List (Rainforest Alliance / Global Forest Watch, Jan 2026) — 2,386 mills globally with GPS coordinates.

Three matching bugs found and fixed across development:

1. Initial code searched only the Group Name column. Many companies list mills under Parent Company instead. SD Guthrie: 2 mills → 42 mills after searching both columns.

2. GAR's UML Group Name is "SINAR MAS" (operating subsidiary). GAR: 3 mills → 50 after fixing the keyword.

3. ~95 UML rows use a non-breaking space character inside Group Name, which a literal-space keyword silently failed to match. All companies reprocessed after normalizing whitespace.

Final verified count: 290 mills across 11 companies (GAR 50, SD Guthrie 42, Wilmar 45, KLK 30, Astra Agro 34, IOI 15, Musim Mas 18, Genting 15, First Resources 16, Bumitama 14, SIPEF 11).

Step 6 — Satellite Forest Loss Analysis

For each of the 290 mills, a 10 km circle is drawn. Hansen data measures how much forest inside that circle was cleared after 31 December 2020 (lossyear ≥ 21).

Why 10 km: PalmWatch standard — fresh fruit bunch typically travels within 10 km of a mill. Buffer sensitivity analysis run at 5–30 km (Results/buffer_sensitivity.csv); ranking is stable.

Why post-2020: EUDR cutoff is 31 December 2020.

Result: 890,168 ha post-2020 loss across 11 companies, 290 mills. Every single mill shows measurable loss — minimum 105 ha. IOI highest intensity at 4,959 ha/mill. GAR highest total at 171,236 ha.

Cross-validation: RADD SAR alerts (Wageningen) provided independent detection cross-check. Descals et al. (2021) oil palm overlay confirmed 56.0% of post-2020 loss near mills is oil palm land cover.

STEP 7Mismatch Scoring (0–100)

Score = 0.35 × Forest Loss + 0.35 × Claim Specificity + 0.15 × Sentiment + 0.15 × Spatial Match. Normalised per forest-2000 baseline. Weights sum = 1.0.

Calamai 2025 Stammbach 2022 Hansen 2013

Step 7 — Mismatch Scoring (Canonical Formula)

Formula (professor-approved, weights sum to 1.0):

Score = 0.35 × Forest Loss (normalised 0–100) + 0.35 × Claim Specificity (0–100) + 0.15 × Sentiment Positive (0–100) + 0.15 × Spatial Match (% mills above median loss)

Forest loss normalised as % of year-2000 forest — not raw hectares — so companies with more mills are not penalised for size.

High score = greenwashing signal: Strong, specific, confident claims + heavy post-2020 clearing.

Final ranking (top 3): KLK 65.6, GAR 63.1, Musim Mas 56.2. Lowest: Astra Agro 17.6 (not because its loss is small — 87,198 ha — but because its claims are few and vague, giving the method little to contradict).

Validation — Is the Score Valid? (4 Cross-Checks)

No labelled ground truth exists — supervised classification is not possible. Four validation approaches:

1. NLP gold-label evaluation: 150 claims manually annotated (Results/nlp_gold_labeled.csv). NLP model comparison across 4 models (Results/nlp_model_comparison.csv). Error rate reported.

2. RADD SAR cross-validation: Independent SAR-based deforestation alert system (Wageningen). Results in Results/radd_validation_summary.csv. Note: RADD tile coverage lower in Sabah; Hansen optical record remains authoritative.

3. Descals oil palm overlay: 56.0% of post-2020 cleared land near mills is confirmed oil palm (Descals 2021). Range: IOI 89.8% to Bumitama 16.6%.

4. Prithvi-EO-2.0 foundation model: IBM-NASA geospatial foundation model evaluated across all 290 mills (Results/prithvi_batch_290.csv). Mean Spearman ρ=0.172 with Hansen loss. Confirms directional agreement. IoU=0.086 (frozen, zero-shot — fine-tuning would improve).

Buffer sensitivity: Scores recomputed at 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 km. Ranking is stable (Results/buffer_sensitivity.csv).

Literature Review

Research papers reviewed and how they relate to this thesis

Foundational Papers — the pipeline runs on these

These are not "selected" — they are load-bearing. The thesis cannot be described without them.

Stammbach et al. (2022) — A Dataset for Detecting Real-World Environmental Claims

arXiv:2209.00507

What it does: Built a labeled dataset of real corporate environmental claims and trained a classifier on it. The model — climatebert/environmental-claims — is publicly available.

How it connects: This IS the NLP model used in Layer 1. Every claim detection in this thesis depends on this paper. Cite every time the NLP pipeline is mentioned.

Layer 1 · Claim Detection

Webersinke, Kraus, Bingler & Leippold (2022) — ClimateBERT: A Pretrained Language Model for Climate-Related Text

arXiv:2110.12010

What it does: Fine-tuned BERT on climate documents (IPCC reports, sustainability filings, climate news). Result: ClimateBERT — a model family that understands climate language far better than general models.

How it connects: Both classifiers used (environmental-claims and climate-specificity) are built on ClimateBERT. This is the base model. Cite when explaining why ClimateBERT models were chosen over general-purpose models.

Layer 1 · Model Base

Araci (2019) — FinBERT: Financial Sentiment Analysis with Pre-trained Language Models

arXiv:1908.10063

What it does: Fine-tuned BERT on financial news corpus. ProsusAI/finbert is the public model. Classifies text as positive/neutral/negative with high accuracy on financial language.

How it connects: FinBERT's positive-sentiment score is the sentiment_score component of the mismatch formula (weight 0.15). Cite when explaining how sentiment enters the score.

Layer 1 · Sentiment

Hansen et al. (2013) — High-Resolution Global Maps of 21st-Century Forest Cover Change

Science, 342(6160), 850–853 · 12,000+ citations

What it does: Maps every 30×30 m pixel of forest loss on Earth from 2001 onwards, updated annually. Now at v1.13 (2025).

How it connects: Every forest-loss figure in this thesis comes from this dataset. Cite for every number — 890,168 ha, 127,606 ha (Wilmar), 6,675 ha (Sabahmas mill) — all from Hansen et al. (2013).

Layer 2 · Satellite Data

EUDR — Regulation (EU) 2023/1115

EU Official Journal · In force December 2026 for large operators

What it does: Requires palm oil (and 6 other commodities) entering the EU market to be deforestation-free after 31 December 2020, proven with GPS-level data.

How it connects: Justifies the entire post-2020 analysis window (lossyear ≥ 21). Gives the thesis direct policy relevance. Cite when explaining why 2020 was chosen as the cutoff.

Policy · Cutoff Date

PalmWatch Methodology (Inclusive Development International + U. Chicago)

palmwatch.inclusivedevelopment.net/about

What it does: Attributes deforestation footprints to consumer brands using mill catchment buffers (10 km) + Hansen data + UML. Publicly documented.

How it connects: This thesis uses the same method but points it at producers not brands, and adds NLP. PalmWatch justifies the 10 km buffer, the UML as a source, and the attribution limitation.

Layer 2 · Method

Descals et al. (2021) — High-resolution global map of smallholder and industrial closed-canopy oil palm plantations

Earth System Science Data, 13(3), 1211–1231

What it does: Provides a 10 m global oil palm land-cover map for 2019, distinguishing industrial from smallholder plantations.

How it connects: Used to overlay post-2020 forest loss near mills. Result: 56.0% of cleared land near mills is confirmed oil palm. IOI highest (89.8%), Bumitama lowest (16.6%). Addresses the attribution gap — confirms land use type after clearing.

Layer 2 · Validation

Recent Papers — justify how the components are assembled

These were selected: one per pillar of the design — NLP method, verification gap, regulatory context.

Calamai et al. (2025) — Detecting Greenwashing: A Natural Language Processing Literature Survey

arXiv:2502.07541 (v2, January 2025) · 61 papers reviewed

What it does: First comprehensive survey of NLP approaches for greenwashing detection. Finds the field is fragmented. Recommends combining third-party evidence (like satellite data) with NLP, and using decomposed pipelines rather than black-box systems.

How it connects: Independently recommends exactly this thesis design. Satellite data = third-party evidence. The 4-layer pipeline = decomposed pipeline. Use in Related Work to prove the research gap and justify the methodology.

Lit Review · Novelty

Ong et al. (2025) — Towards Robust ESG Analysis Against Greenwashing Risks

ACL 2025 (main conference) · arXiv:2502.15821

What it does: Shows that existing ESG NLP approaches — including Stammbach's model — do not account for how greenwashing distorts the results. Claim extraction alone is not verification.

How it connects: Proves the novelty of this thesis. Stammbach detects claims. This thesis verifies them against satellite data. Ong confirms that verification step is missing from prior work.

Lit Review · Novelty

Bingler et al. (2022) — Cheap Talk and Cherry-Picking

Ecological Economics · ClimateBERT applied to 14,584 corporate reports

What it does: Proves that corporate climate language is systematically vague. Companies use non-committal language that requires nothing of them. Only companies under investor/regulator pressure use specific language.

How it connects: Motivates why the specificity score matters — soft claims should not be treated the same as hard ones. When Wilmar shows 4 scored claims, Bingler explains why that pattern exists across the whole industry.

NLP · Motivation

The Research Gap — what no paper has done before

Every paper above does either NLP on corporate text OR satellite deforestation monitoring. No paper combines both to verify each producer's own stated commitments against independent satellite evidence and produce a per-company mismatch score. Calamai (2025) confirms this gap. Ong (2025) confirms verification is missing. This thesis fills that gap specifically for palm oil, using the EUDR compliance window as the measurement frame. Across 290 mills and 11 companies, 890,168 ha of post-2020 forest loss was measured — against claims of zero deforestation.
