1. The Big Picture: Why This Thesis Exists

Over the past decade, multinational consumer brands and palm oil conglomerates faced immense public pressure to end tropical deforestation. In response, virtually every major producer published formal Zero-Deforestation, No Peat, No Exploitation (NDPE) commitments.

However, corporate sustainability auditing suffers from an open verification gap:

  • Text-Only Auditing: Natural Language Processing tools can analyze sustainability reports, corporate filings, and ESG disclosures. They can detect whether the language is confident, vague, or optimistic. However, text models cannot see the Earth. A company can write ambitious commitments while trees are being cleared thousands of miles away.
  • Satellite-Only Auditing: Remote sensing platforms (such as PalmWatch or Global Forest Watch) use Earth observation satellites to measure forest clearing. However, satellite tools do not connect this physical data to what corporate executives specifically promised. They track deforestation footprints without measuring corporate accountability or truthfulness.
The Core Thesis Contribution:
This thesis closes this gap by building an automated, reproducible audit architecture. It pairs an independent Natural Language Processing claims pipeline with an independent Google Earth Engine satellite pipeline, comparing stated commitments against physical observations through an objective Mismatch Score (0 to 100).

2. First Principles & Industry Vocabulary

Every scientific discipline has its foundational vocabulary. In this thesis, data science meets tropical agro-industrial supply chains:

🌴 Palm Oil (*Elaeis guineensis*)

An edible vegetable oil derived from the fruit of oil palm trees. It is the most productive oil crop per hectare in the world. Palm oil is in 50% of packaged supermarket products (chocolate, biscuits, shampoo, cosmetics, soap, and biofuel). Indonesia and Malaysia produce roughly 85% of global supply. Its expansion has driven significant historical deforestation in Borneo, Sumatra, Sulawesi, and New Guinea.

🏭 The Palm Oil Mill & The 24-Hour Rule

The industrial crushing facility located directly inside or adjacent to plantations. Oil palm trees produce heavy fruit clusters called Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB).

The 24-to-48-Hour Freshness Rule: Once harvested, enzymatic acidification begins immediately. If the fruit is not processed within 24 to 48 hours, the oil degrades into Free Fatty Acids (FFA) and becomes commercially ruined. Therefore, fresh fruit bunches cannot be transported across long distances; they must be trucked directly to a local mill. Mills are permanent, known geographic anchor points.

📐 Hectare (ha)

The international metric unit for measuring land area (1 hectare = 10,000 square meters, or a square 100 meters by 100 meters).

Mental Comparison: 1 hectare is roughly 1.4 standard soccer fields.
In this study, you detected 890,168 hectares of post-2020 forest loss across 290 mills. That area is roughly 10 times the size of Berlin, or the entire landmass of Cyprus.

⭕ The 10 km Buffer & Catchment

Around each mill coordinate, code draws a 10 kilometer radial circular buffer (covering 314 square kilometers, or 31,400 hectares). In rural Southeast Asia, plantation trucks hauling heavy fruit bunches can travel roughly 10 to 20 km over dirt roads within the 24-hour freshness window.

This 10 km radius follows the published methodology of PalmWatch (Inclusive Development International). It measures spatial proximity and correspondence, not definitive legal causation, because third-party farmers also operate within that radius.

🌲 Tree Cover Loss vs. Deforestation

Hansen Global Forest Change (GFC) measures biophysical tree cover loss: the removal of canopy cover exceeding 30% density at 30-meter Landsat pixel resolution. It does not determine whether trees were cleared for oil palm, timber, or lost to fire.

Strict deforestation means permanent land-use conversion. To address this difference, the thesis incorporates the Descals et al. (2021) 10m deep learning map to prove what the cleared land became.

⚖️ Greenwashing vs. Cheap Talk

Greenwashing: When a company spends more effort marketing itself as environmentally friendly than actually minimizing its environmental impact. In palm oil, it means claiming "100% No Deforestation" while surrounding forests continue to be cleared.

Cheap Talk: Statements that sound ambitious but are unmeasurable, cost nothing to say, and cannot be falsified (e.g., "We cherish our planet's green heritage").

In-Depth: What Exactly Does "Post-2020" Mean?

1. The Literal Meaning & Time Scope

"Post-2020" means any forest clearing that occurred after 31 December 2020.

In your dataset, this specifically covers calendar years 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025.

Across all 290 mills evaluated in your thesis, this post-2020 filter identified 890,168 hectares of forest loss. Every single mill (290 of 290) recorded post-2020 clearing.

2. Legal Motivation: The EUDR Cutoff Date

The cutoff date of 31 December 2020 was not picked arbitrarily. It is the exact legal baseline established by the European Union Deforestation Regulation (EUDR, Regulation EU 2023/1115).

Under EUDR law, any palm oil, soy, beef, cocoa, coffee, rubber, or timber entering the European single market must be verified deforestation-free:
"Any agricultural commodity produced on land that was deforested after 31 December 2020 is illegal to import or sell in the European Union."

By anchoring the thesis to 31 December 2020, your research evaluates companies directly against the real regulatory standard they face in global commerce.

3. Historical Clearing vs. Post-2020 Ongoing Clearing

Understanding this difference is critical for demonstrating greenwashing:

• Historical Clearing (Before 2021): If a plantation cleared rainforest in 2005 or 2012, corporate executives can defend themselves by claiming: "That occurred years ago before our modern sustainability policies were enacted."

• Post-2020 Ongoing Clearing (2021 to 2025): By 2020, all 11 companies in this study had already enacted public No Deforestation, No Peat, No Exploitation (NDPE) commitments. If trees are cut down near their mills in 2021 through 2025, they cannot claim legacy excuses. It is ongoing clearing occurring while they actively market themselves as 100% sustainable. That is the essence of greenwashing.

3. Remote Sensing & Satellite Science

Remote sensing is the science of acquiring information about the Earth's surface using airborne or satellite-based sensors without physical ground contact.

What is an Earth Observation Satellite?

An Earth observation satellite is an uncrewed spacecraft orbiting the Earth at altitudes typically between 500 and 800 kilometers. As it orbits, digital sensors scan the Earth's surface line by line, taking continuous calibrated multi-spectral photographs. Because satellites revisit the exact same coordinates every few days, they allow researchers to detect landscape changes over time across vast, inaccessible tropical forests without needing ground patrols.

Key Satellite Concepts Explained:

Pixel Resolution: 30-Meter vs. 10-Meter

A satellite image is composed of a grid of tiny square pixels:

• 30-Meter Resolution (Landsat): Each pixel represents an area on the ground measuring 30 meters by 30 meters (900 square meters, or 0.09 hectares). If a single tree falls, Landsat will not see it. But if an area the size of a soccer field is cleared, the pixel value changes dramatically.

• 10-Meter Resolution (Sentinel-2): Each pixel represents 10 meters by 10 meters (100 square meters). It provides 9 times more spatial detail, making plantation roads, drainage channels, small clearings, and concession boundaries clearly visible.

Multi-Spectral Bands & Chlorophyll Reflection

Satellites capture light beyond the visible human spectrum (Red, Green, Blue):

• Near-Infrared (NIR, around 842 nm): Healthy, living green leaves have internal spongy mesophyll cells that reflect near-infrared radiation like a mirror. In a satellite NIR band, healthy rainforest glows intensely. When trees are cut down or burned, NIR reflectance drops sharply to near zero.

• Shortwave Infrared (SWIR, 1610 nm and 2190 nm): Highly sensitive to moisture content. Living forest absorbs SWIR; dry, cleared, bare soil reflects high SWIR. Comparing NIR and SWIR enables automated clearing detection.

Cloud Masking & Median Composites

In tropical equatorial countries like Indonesia and Malaysia, persistent cloud cover and smoke obscure optical satellite views almost daily.

The Technical Solution: Rather than relying on a single cloudy day, Google Earth Engine collects every satellite image captured over an entire year (e.g., 30 observations in 2024). It masks out cloud and shadow pixels using QA quality assessment bands, then calculates the statistical median value for each pixel. This produces a cloud-free, standardized composite of the mill's landscape.

Synthetic Aperture Radar (Sentinel-1 SAR)

Unlike optical satellites that passively capture sunlight, Sentinel-1 is an active radar sensor. It emits microwave pulses (C-band, around 5.6 cm wavelength) toward the ground and measures the returning backscatter.

Microwaves pass directly through clouds, rain, and smoke. When dense tree canopies are cleared, the radar bounce pattern changes immediately. This powers the Wageningen RADD alert system.

Google Earth Engine (GEE) Cloud Platform

Google Earth Engine is a planetary-scale cloud platform for geospatial analysis. Instead of downloading terabytes of satellite imagery to a local computer, code runs on Google's cloud servers:

  • ee.Image("UMD/hansen/global_forest_change_2025_v1_13"): Calls the global multi-decadal forest change raster.
  • ee.Geometry.Point([lon, lat]).buffer(10000): Creates a 10,000-meter circular polygon (314 km2 catchment) around each mill coordinate.
  • reduceRegion(ee.Reducer.sum(), region, scale=30): Computes the spatial sum of all loss pixels inside the polygon at 30-meter resolution.
  • maxPixels = 1e10: Expands the default memory ceiling (from 10 million to 10 billion pixels) to allow batch reductions across 91,000 km2 of mill buffers without memory exhaustion.

  2. Your code extracts the exact GPS coordinates of the company's mills from the Universal Mill List.

  4. The satellite sensor provides an unbiased, physical record showing exactly how many hectares of tree canopy were removed near those mills post-2020.
  5. Your canonical scoring engine compares the specificity of the claim against the measured satellite loss, assigning an objective Mismatch Score.

4. Natural Language Processing (NLP) & Models

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is the branch of artificial intelligence that enables computers to understand, interpret, and extract meaning from human text.

From Text to Transformers

Traditional NLP used simple keyword matching (checking if the word "deforestation" appears). However, keyword search produces heavy false positives (for example, table of contents headings).

Modern NLP uses Transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017) and BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers). BERT processes entire sentences bidirectionally, understanding words in context.

The NLP Model Stack Used in This Thesis:

1. Claim Detection: climatebert/environmental-claims

Pre-trained on corporate climate text and fine-tuned on Stammbach et al. (2022) criteria. Classifies whether a sentence is an active environmental assertion or incidental text. Achieved 97.3% accuracy and 0.981 F1 on your 150-sentence gold benchmark.

2. Specificity Scoring: climatebert/distilroberta-base-climate-specificity

Scores text from 0.0 (vague) to 1.0 (highly concrete). Separates unfalsifiable "cheap talk" (Bingler et al., 2022) from checkable commitments naming dates, targets, percentages, or audit mechanisms.

3. Sentiment Analysis: ProsusAI/finbert

A financial domain transformer model that measures corporate tone, confidence, and promotional optimism. High scores detect self-congratulatory rhetoric that contrasts with physical clearing.

4. ESG Topic Classifier: nbroad/ESG-BERT

Categorizes extracted claims into SASB corporate sustainability topics: GHG Emissions, Ecological Impacts, Energy Management, and Supply Chain Management.

6. The Mathematics & Scoring Formula

Mismatch Score = 0.35 × Forest Loss + 0.35 × Specificity + 0.15 × Sentiment + 0.15 × Spatial Match

Before weighting, every metric is rescaled to a normalized range of 0 to 100 using standard Min-Max normalization:

Normalized Value = [ (Value - Min) / (Max - Min) ] × 100

Why Each Component Matters:

  • Forest Loss Score (35% weight): Normalizes the percentage of baseline year-2000 forest lost inside the company's mill catchments post-2020.
  • Specificity Score (35% weight): Normalizes the mean specificity of corporate commitments. High score = concrete, verifiable promises.
  • Sentiment Score (15% weight): Normalizes the promotional confidence and tone evaluated by FinBERT.
  • Spatial Match Score (15% weight): The percentage of a company's mills that exceed the dataset-wide median per-mill loss (2,653 hectares). A company where most mills are above-median violators receives a higher score than one where clearing is confined to one outlier mill.

Worked Example: Kuala Lumpur Kepong (KLK)

How KLK's Rank 1 score of 65.6 was calculated step-by-step:

1. Forest Loss Score:    33.9  ×  0.35  =  11.9
2. Specificity Score:   100.0  ×  0.35  =  35.0
3. Sentiment Score:      68.4  ×  0.15  =  10.3
4. Spatial Match Score:  56.7  ×  0.15  =   8.5
───────────────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL MISMATCH SCORE:                      65.6  (Rank 1)

Evaluation Metrics Defined in Clean English:

  • Accuracy: (True Positives + True Negatives) / Total Evaluated Sentences. Overall proportion of correct classifications.
  • Precision: True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives). Of all sentences tagged as claims, how many were genuinely claims?
  • Recall: True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives). Of all actual claims in the report, how many did the model find?
  • F1 Score: 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall). The balanced harmonic mean of precision and recall.
  • Cohen's Kappa (κ): Measures agreement between two models above chance (κ = 0 means random agreement; κ = 1 means perfect agreement).
  • Spearman's Rank Correlation (ρ): Measures how well the relationship between two rankings can be described using a monotonic function. Used to evaluate Prithvi change embeddings against Hansen loss fractions.

8. Independent Multi-Sensor Cross-Validations

To prove that findings did not rely on a single satellite algorithm, the thesis implemented four separate validation checks:

1. Foundation Model: IBM/NASA Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M

A 300M parameter Vision Transformer pretrained on multi-spectral satellite sequences. Applied frozen with zero fine-tuning. Tiled Sentinel-2 patches across 2019 (pre-cutoff) and 2024 (post-cutoff). Computed temporal cosine dissimilarity on 768-dimensional embeddings. Reached network-wide mean Spearman correlation rho = 0.172 (p < 0.0001) against Hansen loss across all 290 mills (and rho = 0.597 on IOI Syarimo pilot).

2. Radar SAR Alerts: Wageningen RADD

Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) shoots microwave beams that penetrate equatorial cloud cover and rain. Evaluated on the highest-loss mill across 5 companies. Confirmed active radar disturbance signals at 4 of the 5 mills, validating optical findings through clouds.

3. Land Cover Ground Truth: Descals et al. (2021)

Independent 10-meter deep learning classification of oil palm plantations. Overlaid onto post-2020 Hansen loss pixels. Confirmed that 56.0% of post-2020 clearing inside mill buffers (and 89.8% for IOI) is now oil palm plantation, refuting the argument that clearing was for unrelated crops.

4. Buffer Distance Sensitivity (5 to 30 km)

Tested 5, 10, 15, 20, and 30 km radial buffers around representative mills. Rankings remained stable across adjacent radii (Spearman rho = 0.80 to 0.96). At 30 km (covering 2,827 square kilometers), outside regional noise enters, validating 10 km as the appropriate operational catchment.
