# Chapter 6: Limitations

This chapter states the limitations of this thesis directly, in one place, rather than leaving them scattered across earlier chapters. Section 5.6 touched several of these points already. This chapter restates them with more structure and completes the list.

## 6.1 Proximity is not attribution

The satellite layer measures forest loss inside a 10 km radius around each mill (Section 3.3.2). This buffer approximates a mill's fresh fruit bunch sourcing catchment. It does not establish that the mill's own supply chain caused the loss recorded inside it. Independent smallholders, other companies, and unrelated land-use change can all occur within the same radius. PalmWatch documents this same limitation for its own road-network-refined catchment method (Section 2.3), and this thesis inherits it directly, using a simpler fixed radius instead of PalmWatch's refined boundary. The mismatch score should be read as a measure of correspondence between a company's claims and nearby forest loss, not as proof that any specific hectare belongs to any specific mill's operations.

## 6.2 Small claim samples produce noisier scores

Claim count varies sharply across the 11 companies (Section 4.1). KLK's mean specificity score, the input that puts it first in the mismatch ranking, is built from only 8 scored claims, the smallest sample in the dataset. Wilmar's sample is smaller still, at 4 claims. A mean built from so few observations can shift substantially on one or two unusually worded sentences, in a way a 40-claim sample like GAR's or a 66-claim sample like IOI's cannot. The KLK result should be read as suggestive rather than settled, pending a larger claim sample for low-n companies.

## 6.3 The Astra Agro blind spot

The clearest structural limitation this thesis found is not a data error. It is a property of the scoring design itself. Astra Agro ranks lowest of the 11 companies on the mismatch score, 17.6, despite 87,198 ha of post-2020 loss, the fifth-largest total in the dataset (Section 4.6). Independent reporting documents roughly 17,664 ha of Astra Agro subsidiary concessions overlapping forest zones, unpermitted subsidiaries, and an unresolved land dispute affecting over 6,700 ha (validation_note.md). This is a serious, independently corroborated record. It does not produce a high mismatch score, because Astra Agro's specificity score is 13.8 and its sentiment score is 0.0, both near the floor of the dataset (Section 5.4). The company makes few strong, specific, confidently worded public claims. A score built to measure contradiction between claims and evidence needs a claim to contradict. A company that says little, and says it flatly, gives the method less to work with, regardless of how severe its actual record is. This is a genuine finding about the method, not a flaw to explain away, and it means the mismatch score cannot be presented as a general severity ranking of environmental harm without this qualification.

## 6.4 Single-mill scope of the Prithvi proof of concept

The Prithvi-EO-2.0 comparison in Section 4.7 covers exactly one mill, IOI's SYARIMO site, chosen as IOI's highest-loss location. The correlation with the Hansen ground truth was moderate and positive, Spearman 0.597, but one mill cannot establish that a frozen foundation model's change signal generalizes across the other 289 mills, across different vegetation types, cloud conditions, or clearing patterns. It stands as corroborating evidence for the primary pipeline, not a validated alternative to it.

## 6.5 SAR-based cross-validation coverage gaps

The RADD SAR cross-validation in Section 4.7 returned zero alerts for Wilmar's Sabahmas mill despite the Hansen record showing 6,675 ha of loss at that site across all five study years. This null result points to a specific limitation of SAR-based monitoring that optical methods do not share: RADD tile coverage is uneven across the study region, with lower tile density in Sabah state (Malaysian Borneo) than in Sumatra, and RADD's disturbance detection threshold is calibrated to large or sudden clearing events rather than the steady, small-area annual clearing pattern that Sabahmas shows. A clearing of several hundred hectares per year, distributed across a large buffer, may register clearly in an optical time series like Hansen without producing the concentrated backscatter anomaly that RADD requires to raise an alert. This does not invalidate the Hansen result at that site. It means that for this thesis's purpose of monitoring gradual, sustained forest loss across a large mill network, optical monitoring via Hansen provides more reliable coverage than SAR-based alert systems at their current state of deployment. Future work using Sentinel-1 time series processed outside the RADD alert framework, analysing raw backscatter trajectories rather than waiting for threshold-triggered alerts, may close this gap for the mill types and clearing patterns most common in this dataset.

## 6.6 Other limitations already stated in this thesis

Hansen data measures tree cover loss, not deforestation in a strict legal sense. A pixel can register as loss from logging, fire, storm damage, or plantation conversion, and the dataset does not distinguish the cause (Section 3.3.1). Mill matching, though validated by a 23-test suite, treats remaining unmatched mills as missing data rather than a hidden source of error (Section 3.4.4). And the 2025 loss figure is a partial year for at least one company, so the true 2025 total is likely somewhat higher than recorded (Section 4.2).
